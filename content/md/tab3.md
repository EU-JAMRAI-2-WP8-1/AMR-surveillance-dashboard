### Why does having data-driven national empiric treatment guidance matter?

Data-driven national treatment guidance for common infections is a first step towards ensuring effective, safe, and equitable care. Their absence may lead to poorer patient outcomes, higher costs, and accelerated resistance. Although local adaptation might be necessary, having national guidelines will enable an equal and harmonised healthcare. 
Some benefits include: 
- **Consistency in care** - National guidance ensures that all patients, regardless of location, receive similar levels of care based on the best available evidence.
- **Evidence-based** - National guidelines are typically developed by multidisciplinary teams (e.g. infectious disease specialists, epidemiologists, and pharmacologists) with access to broader data and research.
- **Equity in access** - Local guidance may lack resources or expertise, leading to suboptimal treatment in underserved areas. National guidance ensures equitable access to high-quality care.
- **Facilitating data comparison and research** - National guidance enables consistent data collection across regions, making it easier to monitor trends, evaluate outcomes, and conduct research.
- **Benchmarking** - Hospitals and clinics can compare their practices with national standards, identifying areas for improvement.
- **Cost-effectiveness** - National guidance can negotiate better pricing for recommended antibiotics or diagnostics, reducing costs for the healthcare system.

*Note: Having data-driven national empiric treatment guidance in place does not guarantee that they are implemented and widely used in every clinical setting. However, having guidance in place lays a necessary foundation for Antimicrobial Stewardship activities.*
