### 💡 What can we learn from this?

###### Expanding European surveillance through broader culture material inclusion

🤝🏻 **Policy commitment:** Support EU Council Recommendation on AMR (B5a), ensuring that surveillance of AMR encompasses not only blood/CSF isolates but also all other isolates from clinical microbiology laboratories [condensed].

###### Major trends:

- There is generally a high population coverage and geographical representativeness for pathogens isolated from blood/CSF. This is likely an EARS-Net effect achieved from almost 30 years of collecting surveillance data at the European level. – STRENGTH

- Coverage and representativeness drop rapidly for all pathogens when they are isolated from a culture material other than blood/CSF, such as urine. – GAP

###### Actions for change:

- Use the estimated population coverage to identify countries that have national AST data available that could be used to pilot an expansion of European surveillance into additional culture materials.

- Support countries to include isolates from priority pathogens from more culture material in their national surveillance.

###### Why does expanding surveillance into more culture materials matter?

Monitoring less severe yet common infections, such as urinary tract infections, is vital for understanding community-wide AMR epidemiology and guiding empiric treatment. These infections pose a significant burden to antibiotic misuse.

The EARS-Net effect visible in the data underscores the normative influence of European surveillance on national systems. Simply requesting data creates political pressure and incentivises countries to strengthen collection of data at national level.
